As I stated on the conclusion of my book, *Men that played football for fun The Pinnace Card Collection. A collectors check list*, I would like to have extended the interest in the Pinnace Collection, to a second book, that of the player biographies, using as many of the card characters as possible, from this intriguing collection of antique cards, but the 1000 page plus, finished project, represented outlandish printing costs, set against what I would have asked an enthusiast to pay to own a volume.

So I decided that the next best option was a web site.

To the football enthusiast many players names jump out immediately as you scroll through hundreds of images, Hughie Gallacher, Billy Meredith, Stan Seymour, Elisha Scott and many more who are renown names to the enthusiast, football fan.

I found, as my research progressed, there are characters in this set that are not so well known, but nevertheless, are individual club favourites, military heroes, successful football managers or just outstanding members of society, later in their lives, and they all needed a chance to be re-invented as accurately as possible.

There are unusual names, unusual looking characters, some in Sunday best, but all worthy of bringing back to life, from a bygone age of sporting entertainment.

It has become obvious from my research that many became journeymen, looking to combine their love of the sport, with the needs, of normal life, as Britain re-built its infrastructure as a nation, after the Great War.

It would be fair at this stage to say a little about those who have assisted me in this project. 

From the outset I must thank Roger Pashby owner of the original Pinnace Card Collection web site dedicated to the histrionics of the incredible collection.

Secondly, I would like to thank James Cotton, owner of the web site, Vintage Footballers.

I found James very helpful, in that he used similar initial points of reference,as myself, when collating the player characters information.

I found his biographies very concise but never failing in interesting and relevant points of content.

Both, Roger and James, have openly and unequivocally allowed me to copy and paste, to my research, their images and biographies and neither have tired of giving me support and assistance, when, I have felt the need to ask or seek advice.

Penultimately, I must thank all of the football contributors to Wikipedia who's biographies I have copied and pasted to my information pages.

Finally, I would like to thank my relative, Steven Loscalzo, who helped me when I had lost hope of seeing my three-year project, come to fruition, either online or in print. Steven lives in America and has worked tirelessly to create this very attractive website.

I also wish to thank Wikipedia's football contributors, whose biographies I have copied onto my information pages.

Unfortunately, I cannot name the contributors or seek their permission directly, I have added material and removed details that were irrelevant or did not improve the finished article, but, I hope they welcome this adaptation of their work.

I believe most football enthusiasts today think that large sums have been spent on players only in the modern era.

Using the online, Bank of England's inflation checker, I have converted many of the transfer fees, wages and benefit payments, from the early 1920s into present-day values, obviously the conversions are relevant to the research period of the book (2023-2026), but nevertheless does indicate, to some extent, the comparisons in spending, then and now. I hope you find that an interesting feature of the biographies.

The comparisons reveal the striking sums, clubs were spending on players, even then, while Britain was rebuilding it's infra-structure, after the First World War, and its people were living frugally.
