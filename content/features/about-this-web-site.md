As I stated on the conclusion of my book, Men that played football for fun The Pinnace Card

Collection. A collectors check list, I would like to have extended the interest in the Pinnace

Collection, to a second book, that of biographies, using as many of the players as possible, from this intriguing collection of antique cards, but the 1000 page plus, finished project, represented

outlandish printing costs, set against what I would have asked an enthusiast to pay to own a volume.

So I decided that the next best option was a web site.

To the football enthusiast many players names jump out immediately as you scroll through hundreds of images, Hughie Gallacher, Billy Meredith, Stan Seymour, Elisha Scott and many more who are

renown names to the enthusiast, football fan.

I found, as my research progressed, there are characters in this set that are not so well known, but never the less, are individual club favourites, military heroes, successful football managers or just outstanding members of society, later in their lives.

There are unusual names, unusual looking characters, some in Sunday best, but all

worthy of bringing back to life, from a bygone age of sporting entertainment.

.

It has become obvious from my research that many became journeymen, looking to combine their love of the sport, with the needs, of normal life, as Britain re-built its infrastructure as a nation, after the Great War.

It would be fair at this stage to say a little of the content of this site, and try to qualify the content of the production.

From the outset I must thank Roger Pashby owner of the original Pinnace Card Collection web site dedicated to the histrionics of the incredible collection.

Secondly, I would like to thank James Cotton, owner of the web site, Vintage Footballers.

I found James very helpful in that he used similar initial points of reference for player characters

featured on his site, as myself, many included on the original Pinnace Card Collection.

I found his biographies very concise but never failing in interesting and relevant points of content.

Both, Roger and James, have openly and unaquivably allowed me to copy and paste, to my

research, their images and biographies and neither have tired of giving me support and assistance, when ever I have felt the need to ask or seek advice.

Penultimately, I must thank all of the football contributors to Wikipedia who's biographies I have

copied and pasted to my information pages.

I unfortunately cannot name anyone, or ask directly for permission to use their information, but hope they like to see their efforts, re-invented in this way. I have added too, and taken away some parts that were either irrelevant or didn't enhance my perceived finished article.

Lastly, I think most modern day football enthusiasts view of the game is, that it is only in present day that massive monies have been spent on players.

Using the Bank of England inflation checker, I have been able to show, present day valuations against transfer money, wages and payments in benefits being paid to players in the early 1920's. You will find, not forgetting Britain was trying to, re-build, its population trying to live frugally following WW1, that even then, intriguing amounts of money was being spent by clubs on their players.
