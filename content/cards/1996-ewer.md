## Biography

F.H. Ewer was a great club man and made 160 appearances which was the tenth most in the history of the club. He was born in West Ham on 30th September 1898 and joined the Corinthians and the Casuals in 1922. He played 105 games for the Casuals and it was whilst them that he was awarded his two England full international caps in 8th December 1924 against Belgium a 4-0 win played at The Hawthorns, Wet Bromwich, and 17th June the same year against France a 3-1 win played in Paris, Both matches were friendlies. He also gained 14 amateur international caps between 1924 and 1930. Ewer also featured for the amateurs in the 1924 F.A. Charity Shield. (Initially, this trophy was traditionally played yearly between Professionals and Amateurs)
Ewer played in the wing-half position and could play on either side of the field; he was amongst the leading amateurs of the era and was a resolute, gritty performer, who came back from many injuries and never knew when he was beaten. He later became joint secretary of the Corinthian F.C. in the 1937/38 season.

It seems his early work was as a clerk in the London area a career which his father was already in.

Ewer seems to have had some association with the Military serving as a gunner in the Honorable Artillery Company and the Royal Artillery during WW1, from around 1917.

Much later, following the war, he appears to have stayed with the Royal Artillery until the early 1920's.

He may well have resumed clerical work following these periods but by 1939 he was out of the Army and working as a member of the stock exchange in London.

He married and had two children
