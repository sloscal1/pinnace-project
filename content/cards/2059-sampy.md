## Biography

Originally, Sampy was an engineer by trade, playing his early football in his native Tyneside in the north-east of England, turning out for Choppington, South Shields and Chopwell Colliery before being spotted by Sheffield United. His trade would later be of use to him during WW1 when he became an engineering production manager.

Turning professional and signing for the Blades in November 1920, Sampy was initially employed as an inside right, scoring on his debut against Blackburn Rovers in February 1921. After playing regularly in that role for a number of seasons, Sampy was switched to right half in the autumn of 1925, where he played for the rest of his Blades career.

Sampy was made club captain during the 1930/31 season but lost his place and the captaincy to Harry Gooney a year later. Regaining his place in the first team during the 1932/33 season, Sampy remained a regular with United until 1934.

After 14 seasons and 371 league and cup appearances for Sheffield United, Sampy was transferred to nearby rivals Barnsley in the summer of 1934, taking the role of player-coach. His role was primarily focused on the coaching side however, and Sampy made only one league appearance for the Tykes. In May 1936, Sampy accepted a coaching role at Sheffield Wednesday where he remained until the onset of World War II in 1938.

Thomas Sampy was the elder brother of William 'Bill' Sampy (1901-1973), who also represented Sheffield United. While playing, Sampy had also owned a gentlemen's outfitters on Abbeydale Road in Sheffield.
