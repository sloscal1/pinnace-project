## Biography

Tommy Urwin is one of only four player's to be awarded benefit games at all three of the north east's 'big clubs', in their career.

An England schoolboy International in 1910, he progressed through junior football at Fulwell, Lambton Star and Shildon Athletic before signing for Middlesbrough in 1914 where he became a great servant to the club making 200 league and cup appearances prior to and after WW1.

He was a small but crafty winger blessed with great pace and could play on either wing.

In 1924 Newcastle paid £3,200 (£160,000+ now) for his services, his prowess on the wing continuing, winning a league championship medal in 1927 as part of Newcastle's 'midget forward line' (a media term of the day).

In 1925 at the age of 34 he moved to Sunderland continuing his successful career and completing a unique record of playing for all three of the north east's 'big clubs'. He remained with Sunderland giving 10 years service, as player coach and later scouting, where be combined that task with working as an accountant at Sunderland Royal Infirmary.
