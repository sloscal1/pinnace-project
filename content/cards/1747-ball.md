## Biography

This player is unique, he holds the unenviable title of the only English footballer to be murdered whilst still an active footballer.

Ball played his early football in County Durham being brought up in Usworth now a suburb of Washington, County, Durham.

As a ten year old he won medals whilst a schoolboy.

After school working as a coal miner he played for various colliery teams, before being signed by Newcastle United.

He made no first team appearances for United but moved on to Aston Villa in 1920 being seen as an eventual replacement for Frank Barson, (see card 440} in the collection. Again his appearances were limited until Barson left in 1922.

Following Barson's departure Ball became a regular, performing well and tipped for an England call up.

However, his last game in a Villa shirt was the 10th November 1923 a 1-0 victory against Notts County at Meadow Lane, Nottingham.

Ball had in May 1922, married Beatrice Richards the daughter of a local butchery business owner.

In October they moved into Somerville Cottages, Brick Kiln Lane (now Beeches Road) Perry Bar, Birmingham.

The property was rented from George Stagg a retired Birmingham City Police Officer.

The relationship between Ball and land lord was strained to say the least and for varying reason's.

Stagg objected to Ball's chickens straying and he had threatened to poison them, and made attempts to evict the couple.

Following his final game at Nottingham, Sunday 11th November, Ball and his wife attended the Church Tavern where Ball consumed over 3 pints of Ale.

At 10pm, and now at home, Ball having gone to the garden of his house to retrieve his pet dog got into some sort of verbal disagreement, and a gunshot quickly followed, his wife visiting the area of the commotion was met by Ball staggering into her arms and Stagg who was in the garden, shot again the shot passing over Beatrice's shoulder.

Ball died shortly afterwards, Stagg remaining at the scene until arrested later by the Police.

A lavish funeral took place much later with tributes from Mddlesborough and other football clubs and a collection at a later Villa game raised over £100 (£5,000+ now) for Ball's widow

Ball was buried at St Johns Church under an ornate grave with decoration consisting of Footballs and an inscription from Aston Villa players. It later fell into disrepair but was restored by a modern day Villa enthusiast.

At a trial in 1924, Stagg said the gun went off accidently when a drunken Ball tried to yank the gun away in the struggle that followed the argument.

It was also alleged that during the argument Ball had verbally threatened Mrs. Stagg with violence, she was watching from Staggs nearby house and that Ball sometimes displayed aggression towards his wife.

After deliberation of 1 hour 40 minutes a jury returned a guilty verdict of Willful Murder but recommended Stagg's life should be spared. However, the judge, Justice Rowlett, disagreed and sentenced Stagg to death.

He was refused right to appeal but the sentence was commuted to life in prison following a ruling by newly elected Labour government.

Stagg was subsequently declared insane and following incarceration in Broadmoor he died in February 1966 in a Birmingham mental asylum aged 87 yrs.
