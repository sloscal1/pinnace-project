## Biography

A full back who was renowned for his uncompromising tackling and commitment. It took a while for Walker's career to get going.

He was 20 when he stepped from junior football to Raith Rovers where he had short lived runs in the team.

Retaining his registration they were able to command a fee when he signed for Rangers, in Glasgow in 1905. At Ibrox a similar sequence occurred, in and out of the team, subsequently being allowed to play for Beith in cup games and was eventually loaned out to Cowdenbeath. At Cowdenbeath he was made captain and finally started showing his prowess, sufficient for Swindon Town to buy him from Rangers who still owned his registration.

At Swindon his career blossomed even more, winning the southern League title with them, followed by three runners up finishes, and being FA cup semi finalists twice. His form didn't go unnoticed as 9 Scottish appearances followed between 1911 and 1913.

In the summer of 1913 Middlesbrough paid £1,200 (£114,000+ now) for his services.

As WW1 intervened he was loaned back to Swindon.

In 1917 he was convicted, at Witlshire Assizes, of sexual assault and was sentenced to 12 months imprisonment, with hard labour, but this did not stop him returning and playing for Middlesbrough following his sentence.

A later transfer to Reading followed in 1921, a club, where he finished his career.

After retirement, he applied to become a director at Swindon Town but his application was turned down. He focused his retirement years running a Fish and Chip shop, on Swindon's Manchester Road, where he was to remain for the rest of his life. The junction where the chip shop was is well known in the area and became known as Walkers Corner.
