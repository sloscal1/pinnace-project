## Biography

England 1927 1 appearance, British Home Championship, 12th February 1927, 3-3 draw, v Wales at the Racecourse Ground , Wrexham.

Willie Pease was born on 30 September 1899 in Leeds. His birth name has been mis-reported as William Harold Pease. He was the second of five children.

The arrival and departure of Pease, involved transport problems. He was on the books of Leeds City when the club folded as a club in 1919/20 season amid financial problems, City manager of the time, ex Northampton player, Dick Murrell, recommended Pease to his old club. It was arranged that Cobblers trainer Harry Burrows would meet Pease at Wellingborough railway station but a delay meant there was no public transport still running when he arrived, both having to walk 10 miles to his arranged digs. Despite this issue, next day he trialed and was signed by the club, going straight into the first team, forming a left sided partnership with Louis Page which was said to be the best in the division. It wasn't long before bigger clubs came looking and he moved to Middlesbrough in May 1926, despite visiting scouts, having problems with vehicle breakdowns, getting to the ground to see him play, then finding he had not been selected, the still signed him.

Following good service to Middlesbrough, he moved to Luton Town in June 1933.

Role's as player-manager of Gateshead in December 1934 and a later with Hartlepools United both fell through, and he retired in January 1935.

Pease was married with three sons. After retiring as a player he entered the licensing trade; in 1939 he was the manager of a hotel in Middlesbrough, and in 1940 he was managing a hotel in Stokesley, Yorkshire. He later ran a bed & breakfast in Redcar, where he died on 2 October 1955, aged 56, from a brain hemorrhage.
