## Biography

Francis Carr Hudspeth was an English footballer, who most notably played as a defender for Newcastle United.

Strong dependable left back, solid in all aspects of the position, who formed a great partnership with Billy McCracken in the days of the two defender system, prior to the offside law being changed.

Moving from North Shields for £100 (£9,000+ now), Hudspeth spent nineteen seasons at Newcastle, from 1910 to 1929, this makes him the joint longest servant for the club along with Billy McCracken.

During his time at the club he became a popular figure amongst the fans and gained the nickname 'Old Surefoot' for his reliability. He captained the team from 1923 to 1926, lifting the FA Cup in that period, but was demoted to vice-captain following Hughie Gallacher's arrival at the club. Hudspeth was also known for his ability to score penalties and 25 of his 38 goals at Newcastle came from the penalty spot.

He is second only to Jimmy Lawrence for making the highest number of appearances for Newcastle, taking part in 472 games. He also represented England at international level, v Northern Ireland in 1925-26 season at the age of nearly 36, then making him the oldest debutant player at that time.

At the age of 40 he joined Stockport County there finishing his career.

Post retirement he ran a billiards hall in Newcastle, before going back into football as trainer at Burnley, retiring and living his days out in Lancashire.

Hudspeth served as an able seaman in the Royal Navy during the First World War.

League Championship winner : 1926-27 season.
