## Biography

Clement Ross Voysey was one of a number of players who guested for Leeds City during the First World War. He made one appearance in the 1918/19 season, but his real claim to fame comes from his part in a bizarre episode involving Arsenal some years later. His signing for the Gunners was investigated twice by the FA and was part of the reason why Sir Henry Norris 1865-1934 was banned from involvement in the game, this involved a signing on fee payment demand by Voysey of £200 (£6000+ today) and aspects of his contract at Arsenal, the dates are unclear as is the actual impact on anyone concerned.

Voysey was training to be a teacher when war broke out in 1914 and he joined the Royal Air Force after the introduction of conscription in 1916. He was given an opportunity to play for Manchester City by manager Leslie Knighton (1887-1959) and then, while stationed in Yorkshire, he guested for Leeds City after an injury to regular left-half Bert McLachlan (1892-1956).

Voysey partnered Jack Hampson (1887-1960) and Tommy Lamph ( in the Leeds City half-back line.

Voysey then made a number of appearances for Arsenal, listed as 'Newman' in the programme - something that was common at the time - Officially signing for Arsenal at the end of April, he was thus reunited with Leslie Knighton, now manager at Highbury.

On 17 May 1919, under his own name now, he played in a charity match at Highbury and his performance that day was commented on by two football reporters, paid tribute to some of his playing skills.

But however Voysey was not the finished article, and Football League Division One was not like wartime amateur football. In a match report on Newcastle United 3 Arsenal 1, played on 6 September 1919, 'Athletic News' North East reporter said that although Voysey's distribution out of defence had been very good, he had struggled to impose himself … On 13 September 1919, only a few weeks into his first season as a professional, Voysey got injured … Not only did Voysey not play again for over a year, he needed long treatment at the Great Northern Hospital which the club probably paid for; but after recovery, was never the player he could have been, with injury constantly plaguing his career.

That's not to say that Voysey wasn't a useful member of the Arsenal squad, but it seems he became a fringe player at Arsenal spending periods out of the first team then returning to fill in for more regular players who were injured and sometimes in any position not just his favoured position in defence.

At the end of October 1922 he spent a few weeks on the transfer list, at his own request, after refusing to play in a reserve game; life on the fringes of the first-team was getting him down. He was back in the first team at the end of November, but again it was because first choices were injured, and now he was being used at inside-right. At Christmas 1922 he got injured again and played only a handful of first team games the rest of that season. And so it went on, though at the end of each season Voysey was always offered another contract for next season; which he always signed. Perhaps he hoped that next season he himself would establish or perhaps he found that other clubs were reluctant to sign a player who'd been on the injured list for a season and a half.

In 1925 an FA Emergency Committee was set up to investigate Voysey's contract with Arsenal, though it is unclear who requested the investigation or what was alleged. The timing of the inquiry was odd, with the player having already spent six years at Highbury. The Committee concluded that certain clauses in the contract contradicted one another.

Strangely, Voysey remained at Arsenal despite the findings until Herbert Chapman released him at the end of the 1925/26 season.
