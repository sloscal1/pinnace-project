## Biography

Arguably the most interesting player in the Accrington Stanley card collection.

A global football management/coaching journeyman.

Donnelly played his early Football in the military during the First World War, playing during 1919 for the Royal Artillery platoon he signed for Blackburn Rovers that year, following a recommend from a player, who served with Donnelly in the RA.

His league football journey was a little uneventful playing for the teams listed but in 1928 he joined the newly formed Thames Association Football Club from Custom House London.

They were founded in 1928, and played on a disused ground, which had no football club, in residence.

They were formed by a group of businessmen who had built the West Ham Stadium, with a capacity of 120,000, in the Custom House area of Essex (now part of the London Borough of Newham in Greater London); the stadium was primarily used for greyhound and speedway racing which took place during the week, leaving Saturdays free. The directors of the stadium decided to form a professional football club to play on Saturdays, to bring in additional revenue to the stadium.

They started playing in the Southern League Western Division but were elected to the Third Division South in 1929/30 season in preference to Merthyr Town and ahead of Millwall, Charlton Athletic, Clapton Orient and West Ham. The upgrade in status required an influx of quality players so the club placed an advert "First Class Players Wanted. All Positions" in the June edition of the Athletic News.Thames continued to field a reserve side in the Southern League for a single further season, before withdrawing entirely in 1931.

Donnelly was one of those players but his tenure much like Thames tenure in league football, was not a happy one.

By 1930/31 season and under financial strain despite cost cutting including players taking a wage cut the club decided not to re apply for membership of the league Despite playing in a stadium holding 120,000 people Thames hold the record for the lowest ever recorded attendance.

On the 6th December 1931 only 469 fans attended the stadium to watch them take on Luton Town.

They were replaced by Aldershot.

Donnelly leaving the club embarked on a journey across Europe coaching at many notable clubs :-

Grajanski Zagreb Yugoslavia 1933-1935

Gunnes, Turkey 1936-1937

Amsterdamsche FC, Holland.

AS Ambrosiana-Inter (Inter Milan)

He also coached in Belgium

Returning to England in 1953 he took up a post as player manager with Clitheroe FC in Lancashire.
