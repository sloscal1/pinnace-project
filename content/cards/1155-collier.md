## Biography

Prior to football, Collier was a miner but played football in the military, before seeing action in Egypt in 1917 where he was wounded, and which, also saw his brother Walter killed in action. After the war he signed for Raith forming a formidable defensive line up with Raeburn and Morris (see pages 726 & 728), which culminated in him being capped in 1922. Sadly following his break into the national side his form dwindled and Sheffield Wednesday paid £500 (£ 25,000+ now) for his services in 1924, he was soon dropped from the first team, after only 14 appearances and he moved on to none league football with Kettering Town and Dartford. In 1930, he became manager for Dartford where he discovered Eddie Hapgood (1908-1973) who subsequently went on to fame with Arsenal and England.

Collier resigned in 1937 to return to Dunfermline, where he became licensee of the Unicorn Bar. His brother John Collier (1897-1940) was a footballer playing for Hull and York City. Confusingly, he had a cousin called Bill Collier (no stats) who also played for Raith before emigrating to Canada.
