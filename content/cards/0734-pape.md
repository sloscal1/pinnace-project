## Biography

Elsecar, Yorkshire born centre forward Albert Pape was with junior club Wath Athletic prior to First World War during which he served in The Yorkshire Light Infantry and played for the regimental football team, returning to play for Bolton-on-Dearne at the cessation of hostilities. In December 1919, Pape was signed by Rotherham County, who had been elected to the Second Division at the start of the season, and he made a goalscoring Football League debut on 17th January 1920 in a 4-3 win over Coventry City. In four seasons with Rotherham County, Pape scored 42 league and cup goals in 117 appearances, including a spell in the 1922-23 season in which he scored eight goals in five matches finishing as the club's top scorer in both 1921/22 and 1922/23, when they were relegated, with hat-tricks in a win over Notts County in April 1922 and at Hull City the same October.

He signed for First Division club Notts County at the end of that season, but made just 8 appearances in 1923/24 scoring 3 goals before moving on to Second Division club Clapton Orient. In eight months with Orient, he scored 11 goals in 25 appearances. In February 1925, Orient travelled to a match against a Manchester United side that had just sold its star striker, Bill Henderson (1878-1945), to Preston North End. United manager John Chapman (1882-1948) had telephoned the Orient manager Peter Proudfoot (1879-1941) before they left London, and the two clubs agreed a fee of £1,070 (£54,000+ now) for Pape. They met up at Manchester Piccadilly station just after noon, and Pape – who was a friend of the United captain Frank Barson (1891-1968), and had relatives in nearby Bolton – quickly agreed terms.

The details were wired to The Football Association and The Football League at around 1:30 p.m., and although Pape had been named in Orient's starting line-up for the match, he was confirmed as a Manchester United player with about an hour left before kick-off. Pape was not only allowed to start the match in the colours of Manchester United, but he also scored the team's third goal in a 4-2 win over his previous employers, as well as hitting the post with a header late in the game. He made 15 further appearances that season, and scored four more goals. He also made two appearances in 1925/26, but he was then sold to Fulham in October 1925. However, he was reluctant to return to London, and only signed with Fulham on the condition that he could continue to live in Bolton and train with Manchester United.

Five months later, the two clubs met in the Sixth Round of the FA Cup, and although Pape scored, Manchester United won the match 2-1. After two seasons with Fulham, in which he scored 16 goals in 48 appearances and was the club's top scorer in 1926/27, Pape dropped out of League football to join North Wales coast side Rhyl Athletic, but he was there for less than six months before joining Hurst back in Manchester for the second half of the 1927-28 season. He scored at a rate of almost one goal a game, and even scored a hat-trick on his debut against Port Vale Reserves in the Cheshire County League. Towards the end of the season, the club suffered a goalkeeping injury crisis, and Pape was forced to play one match in goal.

In September 1928, Pape was named as player-coach at Darwen, and was also made club captain. However, five months later, he was signed by Manchester Central, before returning to League football for the start of the 1929/30 season with Hartlepools United. He scored 21 goals in 38 appearances for Hartlepools United in the Football League Third Division (North), and was signed by Halifax Town for one final season of League football in July 1930, scoring 16 goals in 29 appearances in 1930/31. He then spent time with Burscough Rangers, Horwich RMI and Nelson before retiring from football.

NB : According to Fulham a Complete Record 1897-1987 by Dennis Turner & Alex White, Breedon Books, Pape whilst at Fulham was not allowed to play league football between 1927 to 1929 as Fulham held his registration. So, it would seem there could have well have been some contractual dispute going on, possibly relating to Pape moving to another club.

As such whilst a lot of the information in the main body of this bio is probably correct, it may well not have happened in the date order displayed.
