## Biography

Leaving Airdrie for Bradford City in May 1921 for a then club record fee of £3,050 (£126,000+ now), he became an instant success.

But, it could have turned out so different had it not been for the leniency of a Bradford Magistrate in January 1921, who believed his explanation of 'ghost hunting', to a charge of 'found on enclosed premises with intent to commit a felony', was believed. He and fellow Scots Jock Ewart (1891-1943) and Tommy Robb(1899-????), incurred the wrath of Bradford City manager David Menzies for the stupid prank.

Following this, Watson settled down, giving 10 good years to City, before leaving for Walsall in 1931.

It was a sad, that only 2000 spectators turned up, to an arranged benefit game against a Scottish XI in April 1926.

On retirement Watson ran a pub in the village of Baildon, where he would pass away in October 1951.
