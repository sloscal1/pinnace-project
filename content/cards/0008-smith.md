## Biography

Between 1922 and 1928 Smith made 3 appearances for England without scoring

Huddersfield Town: FA Cup: 1921–22, First Division : 1923–24, 1924–25, 1925–26, FA Charity Shield: 1922.

He is one of Huddersfield's all-time top goal-scorers with 130 league and cup career goals, between 1913 and 1934. Smith scored Huddersfield's winning goal in the 1922 FA Cup Final against Preston North End at Stamford Bridge. He also won three caps for England. He was also the first person ever to score from a corner in a match against Arsenal in October 1924. He made 574 appearances for the Terriers.

For a Huddersfield legend, the story opened in an unlikely manner. Seemingly, although no other reference exists, Smith must have trialled at Huddersfield prior to signing a full time contract, and, it was September 1913 when at an Huddesfield board meeting a letter from W.H. Smith (Billy Smith) of Tantobie in County Durham was discussed. The content of the note was short and to the point for the writer made it clear he was not prepared to accept terms of less than 50/- a week (approx £187 now) and for that reason did not wish to present himself to further trials.

Obviously he did, pursue further trials and subsequently signed.

During a Division I match against Stoke he had a fight with a Stoke player. They were both sent off and it meant they would both miss a game, for Smith it meant missing the 1920 FA Cup Final against Aston Villa, despite an Huddersfield appeal. Huddersfield lost the cup final 1-0 as Billy Kirton scored in extra time.

However in1922 he did play in an F.A. Cup final. During the match, Smith was brought down on the edge of the area, the referee awarding a spot kick. (VAR eons in the future) Smith, shaken stepped up himself to take the penalty. Preston goalkeeper Mitchell jumped around on his line in attempt to distract Smith but Clem Stephenson said, "Never mind the dancing devil, just shove it in the net. Smith did."

Smith was a key member of the team that won a hattrick of First Division titles under Herbert Chapman and Cecil Potter and in 1926 received his first England cap. He was one of five Huddersfield players who played in the "Wembley Wizards" 5–1 defeat of England. He, Bob Kelly, Roy Goodall and Tom Wilson played for England, and Alex Jackson for Scotland; Jackson scored a hat-trick, while Kelly scored a consolation for England in the 89th minute.

He later had a short player/manager stint at Rochdale.

He faded out from the game in the 1940s and died of cancer in 1951, after having his left leg amputated due to a mistreated football injury.

He and his son William Conway Smith 1986-1989 were the first father and son to score 100 goals each. His grandson Robert (no record available) played in the last ever match at the Leeds Road ground before it was knocked down.
