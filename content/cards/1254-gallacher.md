## Biography

Not enough pages to talk about this player, a goal scoring legend with a somewhat complicated personal life that became his nemesis.

Small for a centre forward, but quick, strong and always alert to a scoring chance he used his squat robust physique to unsettle much bigger and stronger central defenders.

Gallacher represented Scotland on 20 occasions scoring 24 goals and was in a Scottish League representative side twice in 1925, scoring 5 goals in one of these games, beating the Irish League XI 7-3.

His first cap came against Ireland in 2-0 win on the 1st March 1924 and was a member of Scotland's 1928 Wembley Wizard team.

Gallacher was married twice, with his first wife they had two children, one dying at birth and the second Jackie Gallacher 17/05/1924 becoming a prolific goal scorer for Glasgow Celtic.

He would later remarry and have two more sons, Hughie Jnr, and Mattie (Matthew) his second wife dying of heart disease in December 1950.

Following his retirement and with no savings from his football career Hughie tried his hand at many careers, some menial, to earn money to support his family, including a stint in journalism but was banned from St James Park Newcastle by the club following disparaging remarks made about the team in his press reports.

He continued to turn out regularly in charity event matches even at the grand age of 52 years.

Following a domestic incident in May 1957 involving his son, Gallacher in a fit of anger (possibly whilst under the influence) threw a shoe at Mattie injuring him, following the Police being alerted by a concerned neighbour he was arrested, Mattie was removed from the family home and Gallacher started to experience an emotional mental reaction to the assault, particularly later, when he was summoned to court to be dealt with, for the incident, at Gateshead Magistrates Court in June 1957.

He became irrational and emotional often being seen wandering the streets in an unkempt manner.

On the 11th June he posted a short message to Gateshead Coroners office expressing his regret for the incident and that if he lived to be 100 he would never forgive himself.

Later that day, two train spotters, later, reported seeing Hughie in an agitated state at Low Fell Railway bridge Gateshead which spanned the main London to Edinburgh railway line, pacing the area, he was in an agitated state talking to himself and occasionally pounding the wall of the bridge.

He would eventually step down from the bridge walk way and onto the tracks and into the path of an oncoming express train.

Following this, Mattie moved back into the family home to stay with his brother Hughie Jnr before emigrating to South Africa.

Gallacher has only ever been honoured at Pride Park Derby with a stone built memorial, it is thought that in time Newcastle United may follow suit.
