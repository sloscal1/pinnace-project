## Biography

Lawson had to wait until the end of WW1 before he could embark on a career in football.

A nippy outside right he was playing for Kirkintilloch Rob Roy when he enlisted with the 14th Highland Light Infantry.

In November 1917, serving on the front line, in France, at the Battle of Cambrai, he was captured and became a prisoner of war for a year, being released as the conflict finished.

Returning to England, he joined Kilsyth Emmett, where he started attracting interest of bigger professional clubs, Glasgow Celtic giving him a trial before St Mirren signed him, in November 1918.

In 1921 St Mirren finished last but escaped relegation, as the Scottish leagues, reorganised, and expanded.

So impressive were his performances Scotland picked him to play in a 2-2 draw against England on the 14th April 1923 at Hampden Park, in front of 71,000 fans.

Later that year Cardiff City paid an hefty fee for his services, but his form dwindled and became so disillusioned with the financial outlook at Cardiff.

In September 1926 he headed for the USA where work was in abundance and they were paying extraordinary wages to play football. Unfortunately, this also, was not a success, returning to England less than a year later.

On returning he found it difficult to find a club, signing for Wigan Borough before returning north playing for Clyde then Brechin City in Scotland.

He retired in 1931 and became an engineers checker spending the remainder of his life in Glasgow
